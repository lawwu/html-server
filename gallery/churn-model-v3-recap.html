Churn model v3 is live.

Every Monday the model ranks all subscribers by their chance of cancelling in the next 30 days. The top 2,000 become the retention team's call list for the week.

  • Live since Mon 26 Oct
  • Replaces v2, in use since March 2024
  • Ran 4 weeks side by side before launch

What it bought

Same 2,000 calls a week. 240 more of them now reach someone who was about to cancel.

+44%

That's 39 in every 100 calls, up from 27 — from the same team, in the same hours.

It sees two new warning signs

Failed payments and support contacts. v2 couldn't see either, and they account for most of v3's gain.

It weighs signs together

v2 added the same points whoever the customer was. v3 asks questions in a chain, so a failed payment fixed within days barely counts, and one left unfixed counts a lot.

Technically: gradient-boosted trees (about 400 small decision trees, each correcting the ones before it) replace logistic regression.

Its scores mean what they say

When v3 says 50, about half of those customers cancel within 30 days. A v2 50 meant about a quarter: its scores had crept up over two years.

Recalibrated: checked band by band against who actually cancelled.

Watch-outs

Support data is new

Support contacts only go back to March, when the help desk changed systems. The model learned them from four months of data and hasn't seen a holiday season yet.

Scores can go stale

Customers and prices change. We check every Monday that scores, reasons and results hold steady. One to watch: a bank reissuing cards can fill the list with failed payments from people who aren't leaving.

Live proof takes 30 days

The numbers above come from past weeks. Live results arrive in early December, 30 days after the first v3 list.
