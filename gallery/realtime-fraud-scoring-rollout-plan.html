ML Platform · Rollout plan

Real-time fraud scoring — rollout plan

Move the card-fraud model txn-risk v7 from nightly batch scoring to scoring each authorization as it happens, inside a 50 ms p99 budget. Five phases run in a fixed order; each has an owning role, its dependencies, and checks that must pass before the next one starts.

Why. v7 scores yesterday’s authorizations overnight, and its card risk tier reaches the rules engine at 06:00 B1. A compromised card can be used for most of a day first: in Q3, 61% of confirmed fraud loss on cards v7 later flagged happened before the next 06:00 load B2. Scoring at authorization time puts v7 into the decision itself B3.

Rollback

Until Phase 5, rolling back is one switch. The rules engine’s kill switch B16, per segment or for everything, takes the score out of the decision; those authorizations go back to the batch risk tier, which is today’s behavior B6. Holdout cards never leave it B8.

PhaseBack out byDecisions then use
1 · Feature parityStopping the stream writersThe batch tier, as before
2 · ShadowSwitching the shadow call offThe batch tier, as before
3 · CanaryKill switch for the segmentThe batch tier, immediately
4 · RampKill switch per segment, or for everythingThe batch tier, immediately
5 · RetireRe-enabling the nightly job and the 06:00 loadThe batch tier from the next 06:00 load; the Q2 fallback until then

Phase 5 is the only rollback that isn’t immediate, which is why it waits for the last 60-day read.

Who, when, and what next

  • Who: whoever is on call for the authorization path (SRE or Authorization Platform), without a change ticket. Rolling back restores today’s behavior, so it is allowed during the freeze. B17 Added
  • When: A2 breached for 30 minutes; the fallback rate over the Q5 ceiling; A3 breached on a daily read; A4 failed at a 14- or 60-day read; or a severity-1 or -2 incident on the authorization path involving the scorer. Added
  • Next: the step stays off until its owner and the Fraud Strategy lead agree the cause and its exit checks pass again. Added

From the brief

  1. Q1

    Which merchant segment is the canary?

    The brief leans towards digital goods, card-not-present: about 3% of authorizations, small tickets, and the biggest share of the overnight losses. It is also the noisiest segment for fraud loss, which is what Q3 asks about.

    Answers: Fraud Strategy lead with the ML Platform lead · Needed before Phase 3 · B22

  2. Q2

    What does the rules engine fall back to once the batch tier is gone?

    Candidates, none agreed: the card’s last real-time score held in the online store; rules only; a reduced nightly job that builds only the tier. Added

    Answers: Authorization Platform with Fraud Strategy · Needed before Phase 5 · B24

  3. Q3

    Is a canary of about 3% of authorizations enough to read the fraud-loss guardrail in 14 days?

    Needs the analyst’s estimate of the smallest difference the canary can detect at 14 days. If that is too coarse, the choices are a longer canary or a wider one.

    Answers: fraud strategy analyst · Needed before Phase 3 · B23

  4. Q4

    Do decline thresholds need recalibrating for transaction-level scores, and who signs them off?

    Today’s rules use the card tier. Phase 2’s shadow scores give the data; sign-off could sit with Fraud Strategy or with Model Risk.

    Answers: Fraud Strategy lead and Model Risk · Needed before Phase 3 · B25

Raised while drafting, not in the brief

  1. Q5

    What timeout does the rules engine put on the scoring call, and what fallback rate is acceptable?

    The brief sets the p99 and the fallback but neither number. The fallback-rate alert in R4 needs the second.

    Answers: Authorization Platform with SRE · Needed before Phase 3 · B5 B6

  2. Q6

    Is score-level parity enough?

    A feature can drift without moving today’s scores much. Per-feature tolerances would catch it earlier (R1).

    Answers: fraud data scientist with the feature-pipelines engineer · Needed before Phase 1 ends · B10

  3. Q7

    What else reads the batch scores?

    The brief names the review queues. Reporting and model monitoring may read them too, and would break when the job retires.

    Answers: ML Platform lead · Needed before Phase 5 · B21

Assumptions

What the plan takes as true. If one proves wrong, the phase that leans on it changes.

  • Normal peak is about 240 authorizations a second; last Black Friday reached three times that, about 720. B20
  • About 70% of fraud is reported within 14 days, so the 14-day read sees most of it. B13
  • Digital goods is about 3% of authorizations, the rest of card-not-present about 35%, and card-present about 62%. B7 B22
  • The authorization event stream carries every field the 34 windows need; Phase 1 checks this first. Added
  • The 12 merchant and account lookups change slowly enough to refresh daily, as the batch job does today. Added

Notes on structure

Where this plan interprets the brief rather than restating it:

  • Phases are numbered because the brief fixes their order; 4a and 4b are its own two segments. B7
  • The 14-day read gates every step that changes decisions, not only the canary. B13
  • Phase 5 waits for the last 60-day read: retiring the job ends the holdout, and with it the control that read needs. B8 B13
  • Each phase has one owning role; “With” lists the roles whose work it needs. B9
  • The brief’s four undecided points are Q1–Q4. Q5–Q7 came up while drafting and are kept apart from them.
  • Anything marked Added can be struck without contradicting the brief, and none of it is agreed.
