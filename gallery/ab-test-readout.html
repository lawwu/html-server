Did the onboarding checklist lift 7-day activation?

Yes, by about two points. New users who got the checklist activated at 32.85%, against 30.77% without it: +2.08 percentage points (95% CI +1.17 to +2.99), a 6.8% relative lift. The split was clean and both guardrails held, so the rule we fixed before launch says ship.

Can we trust the split?

Each new account was assigned at sign-up, 50/50, by a hash of its ID. If assignment or event logging were broken, the arms would drift apart in size, a sample ratio mismatch, and nothing else on this page could be trusted. They didn’t drift.

Primary metric: 7-day activation

Activated means that within 7 days of signing up, the new user created a project and invited at least one teammate. That is the standing company definition, unchanged for this test. Every user had the full 7 days: the last window closed on Sun 25 Oct.

That is z = 4.50, p < 0.001. In plain numbers, about 21 more activated users per 1,000 sign-ups (12 to 30), or roughly 60 a day at the current ~2,900 sign-ups a day (34 to 87).

The test was sized to detect +1.3 pp with 80% power in 14 days. The estimate clears that easily. The interval’s lower end (+1.17) sits just under it, so a lift a little smaller than we designed for is still plausible. No lift at all is not.

Guardrails

A guardrail catches a win that costs something elsewhere. Each has a margin, the most harm we agreed in advance to tolerate, and passes only if its whole 95% interval stays on the safe side of it. The hatched zone is the side that fails.

Segments: hypotheses, not verdicts

Seven segments and three metrics make 21 intervals. At 95% each, expect about one to mislead you even if the checklist did exactly the same thing for everyone. Read segments for what to test next, not for decisions.

Decision and next steps

Ship the checklist to all new sign-ups and keep 5% back as a long-term control. The lift is real and large enough to matter, the trust checks pass, and nothing we measured got worse.

  1. Mon 2 NovRamp to 95% of new sign-ups with a 5% holdout, and ship per-step completion logging in the same release so the next version knows which step does the work.Growth eng
  2. 2–13 NovWatch support contacts daily. Roll back if the share of new users contacting support goes above 5.4% (the 4.67% baseline plus the 0.7 pp margin) for a week.Support ops
  3. Mon 14 DecRead the holdout. Compare 28-day retention for the first two weeks of ramped sign-ups: it tests whether checklist-driven activations last.Growth analytics
  4. Q1 planningOnly if Android matters to the roadmap, pre-register an Android test sized to detect a difference in lift. Don’t act on today’s Android number.Onboarding PM

What this test can’t tell us

  • Whether the lift lasts. Two weeks can’t separate a durable effect from novelty.
  • Whether these activations are worth as much. The checklist’s steps are the very actions activation counts, creating a project and inviting a teammate, so some of the lift may be box-ticking. The holdout’s 28-day retention is the check.
  • How the effect varies by segment. The test wasn’t powered for that; see above.

Method notes

Design
Two arms, assigned 50/50 at sign-up by hashed account ID and analysed as assigned, including users who dismissed the checklist.
Window
Sign-ups from Mon 5 to Sun 18 Oct 2026, two full weeks so each weekday counts twice, with every outcome observed for 7 days.
Intervals
Change in proportions with an unpooled normal interval, d ± z·√(p₁q₁/n₁ + p₂q₂/n₂), and p-values from the same z. Relative lift uses the delta method on the log of the ratio.
Sample ratio
χ² goodness of fit against 50/50 with 1 degree of freedom, flagged below p = 0.001.
Segments
The same interval within each segment. The adjusted view is Bonferroni across the 7 segment intervals of the chosen metric (z = 2.69, so 99.3% each). The interaction test is Cochran’s Q across a grouping’s segment estimates.
Fixed in advance
Primary metric, guardrails and margins, the decision rule, duration, and the segment list (as exploratory), all recorded on Fri 2 Oct 2026, before launch.
