Average anything enough times and you get a bell

Pick a distribution that looks nothing like a bell curve. Draw n values from it, take their mean, drop that mean into the histogram on the right. Repeat. The central limit theorem says the pile of means will approach a normal curve centred on μ with spread σ/√n — whatever shape you started with.

Watch the skewed source. At n = 1 the "means" are just draws, so the histogram copies the exponential's long right tail. By n = 5 the tail has shrunk, and by n = 30 the histogram and the dashed normal curve are hard to tell apart — but look closely and a faint right skew survives. How fast the bell arrives depends on how lopsided the source is.

Watch the width. Quadrupling n halves the spread: σ/√n. That square root is why polls need four times the respondents to halve their margin of error.

What it needs. Independent draws from one distribution with a finite variance. A heavy-tailed source like the Cauchy distribution has no finite variance, and its sample means never settle down at all.

All data are generated in the browser from the named distributions; nothing is loaded. The dashed curve is the normal density N(μ, σ²/n) scaled to the histogram.
