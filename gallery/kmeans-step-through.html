What just happened

Two moves, repeated. Lloyd's algorithm alternates an assign step — give each point to its nearest centroid — and an update step — move each centroid to the mean of the points it owns. Each move can only lower the inertia (or leave it unchanged), and there are finitely many ways to partition the points, so the loop must stop.

Stopping is not winning. It stops at a local optimum. Try “all in one corner” with several blobs: centroids crowd together and one cluster can end up straddling two blobs. k-means++ spreads the starting centroids out by picking each new one with probability proportional to its squared distance from those already chosen, which is why it is the default in most libraries.

Choosing k. Inertia always falls as k rises — with k equal to the number of points it is zero — so it cannot pick k by itself. Set k above and below the true number of blobs and compare where the inertia lands; the “elbow” where adding a cluster stops helping much is the usual heuristic.

What k-means assumes. Euclidean distance and a mean as the centre, so it prefers round, similarly-sized clusters. Add a long thin line of points by clicking and watch it get chopped into pieces rather than kept whole.

Synthetic data: Gaussian blobs from a seeded random generator, regenerated with “New data”. No external data is used.
