Model monitoring — three wireframe directions

The screen an on-call data scientist opens when the store demand forecast may be unhealthy. It has one job: answer Is the model healthy, and if not, why? Each direction carries the same scenario and content; only the structure changes.

Who
On-call data scientist, arriving from a page
When
Tue Nov 3, 07:42 PT. Store demand forecast v4.2.1: the 04:00 run is done, three alerts are open, and forecasts publish to replenishment at 10:00.
Shows
Data freshness per source · feature drift (PSI) · prediction distribution shift · accuracy vs actuals (MAPE by region; actuals land 2 days after the sales day) · active alerts · recent deploys and data changes
Answer key: what a good screen should let the on-call conclude
  • Today's forecasts for promoted items are inflated. The promo feed switched discount from 0–1 to 0–100 at Mon 21:05, and promo_depth drifted (PSI 0.41).
  • The late weather file and Mountain's slow MAPE rise are real but separate: data platform has acked the first, and four new stores explain the second.
  • Friday's model deploy is not the cause, and accuracy can't show the problem until Thu Nov 5. Holding the 10:00 publish beats rolling back the model.

Judge each direction by how fast it gets there, and by what it might let the on-call get wrong.

Annotations

  1. “Likely cause” is matched by timing (a change in the 24 h before an alert), not by data lineage. Is that strong enough for the headline?

  3. Holding the publish leads because it beats the 10:00 deadline; rollback sits second because the deploy came days before the problem.

Tests
Whether putting changes above symptoms on one time axis answers “why” directly, and whether drawing the unscored days makes the actuals lag impossible to misread.
Trade-off
Strongest on cause, weakest on what is broken right now, which falls to the summary strip. The answer depends on the window, and per-source and per-feature detail sit a click away.
Phone

Annotations

  1. The window defaults to 7 days so Friday's deploy is on screen and can be ruled out. At 72 h it would be off the left edge.
  2. The screen never names a cause: the Mon 21:05 change sits one column before the Tue jumps. Is alignment enough at 3 a.m.?
  3. The 2-day actuals lag shows up as unscored days, not as a footnote.

Direction A on a phone

The on-call usually opens this from a phone notification, so the triage list is the one that has to work at 360 px. Same markup as A above; scroll inside the frame.

Phone · 360 × 640scrolls inside

The phone view is built from Direction A with JavaScript.

What changes at phone width

  1. Status, the one-line cause and the 10:00 deadline pin to the top. The accuracy caveat moves into Not alerting.
  2. Alerts become full-width cards; only the critical one opens its evidence.
  3. The change just before each alert stays inside it, so the “why” survives the collapse.
  4. Not alerting and Recent changes shrink to one-line disclosures.
  5. Actions move to a bottom bar within thumb reach. Per-alert Ack becomes Ack all (2); Roll back moves under More, one tap further on purpose.

Lost on a phone: evidence and the change list side by side, and the version and run details in the header.

B and C, not drawn: B's tiles stack in pipeline order; C's lanes become a day-by-day feed. Narrow the browser to watch both reflow.

Questions for the review

  • Should the screen name a likely cause? A does, by timing; C lets the alignment speak; B leaves it to the reader.
  • Where does “accuracy can't see this yet” belong: a state on the signal (B), unscored days (C), or a line in the verdict (A)?
  • Is holding the 10:00 publish this screen's primary action, or should it hand off to data platform first?
  • Do routine changes, like the weekly price update, belong in the change list?
  • Could A be the landing view with C one click away, or is that two screens to keep in sync?

Deferred to the mockup

  • Status colors. Severity is carried here by fill, outline and dash alone.
  • Chart forms for PSI history, the prediction histogram and MAPE trends; placeholders and plain numbers stand in.
  • Type, density, icons and brand.
  • What Ack, Hold publish, Roll back and the window control actually do; here they only show placement.
  • Deep links from the paging tool, and how thresholds are explained.
