Accuracy can fool you

Spam is rare here: 10 emails in 100. So a filter that flags nothing is already 90% accurate.

Same score, and the bottom row is the 10 mistakes in both. Only one filter catches spam. Precision and recall show the difference; accuracy hides it.

So where should the line go?

Where mistakes cost least. That depends on which mistake hurts more.

false alarms × 5 slipped through × 1

Many tools put the line at 0.5 by default. That is a habit, not an answer. Spam filters usually lean right: a job offer lost in the spam folder hurts more than one junk email. A filter screening a bank’s staff for phishing should lean left.
